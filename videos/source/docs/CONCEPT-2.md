# Episode 2 concept: "Why the Best Players Look Slow"

Director's brief. Audience: U12–U16 players. Same series, same world, same kit.

## The big idea

Football is not a game of speed. It is a game of time. The best players look slow
because they bought their time before anyone noticed. Time is something you can buy.

Three ways to buy time, each with real science and a real drill:

1. **LOOK** — buy time before the ball arrives. Scan: turn your head and take snapshots
   while the ball is still travelling. Research counts pros' head turns (about 0.6 per second
   in midfield). More scans before receiving means more forward passes and turns. The
   reason: reacting after the ball arrives costs a quarter of a second plus a decision.
   Knowing beforehand costs nothing at the moment that matters.
2. **TOUCH** — buy time as it arrives. Physics: the ball only bounces off the speed
   *difference* between it and your foot. A foot that gives way (moves back with the ball)
   kills the bounce. Then the direction: one touch into space, away from the defender, adds
   metres, and every metre is time (about 0.2 s per metre against a chasing defender).
   One touch that says "I already know where I'm going".
3. **SHAPE** — buy time with your body. The half-turn / open body: your eyes see about a half
   circle. Face the passer and half the pitch is behind you. Stand side-on and you see the
   ball and the goal in one view. Receive on the back foot and the turn is already done.

Bonus teaser: **steal time**. A defender needs about a quarter of a second to react. A body
feint spends his quarter second. (Sets up a possible episode 3 on 1v1.)

## The motif: the time bubble

A glowing ring around Tavi with a number: the seconds until the nearest defender reaches
the ball. It is physically honest: distance to the defender divided by his closing speed.
Cold open: 0.6 s. Ending: the same pass, 2.4 s. "Same legs. Same speed. Four times the time."
Snapshots (small polaroid frames) pop each time Tavi scans; they build a map in a thought
bubble.

## Story

The next night. Nine o'clock again, same floodlit pitch. Tavi brought a friend: **Sam**, who
passes the balls in. **Chalk** is back, but out of the goal tonight: he chases. Cold open:
Sam passes, Tavi's back is to the goal, the ball bounces off the shin, Tavi looks up, Chalk
is already there. "You had the ball for two seconds. It felt like none. Where did the time
go?" Then the bubble reveal, the three chapters, the feint teaser, and the callback: the same
pass, Tavi scans twice while the ball travels, half-turned, cushions it with the back foot
into space, Chalk arrives to nothing. Lights out.

## Rules that carry over

- Simple words (U12–U16), no AI-sounding words, 4–6 learn-words on word cards
  (candidates: scan, first touch, half-turn, back foot, reaction time, cushion).
- Only verified numbers. Show, then explain. Kurzgesagt pacing.
- Every chapter ends with a cue, the classic mistake and its fix, and a drill a player can
  do alone or with one friend, with a safety line.
- Code-drawn SVG. Physics-driven ball. Voice: Charon. Music: Lyria, new tracks in the same
  key family. Target 6:00–6:45.

## New kit needs

- Top-down player tokens with facing and a vision cone (Tavi, Sam, Chalk).
- Time bubble ring + seconds readout (from distance / closing speed).
- Snapshot frames and a thought-bubble map.
- First-touch collision: rebound = e × (ball speed − foot speed) + foot speed.
- Ground passes: rolling ball with realistic slowing on grass.
- A general top-down pitch area (not only around the goal).
- Player colour variants (Sam's shirt) and a "receive" pose family (cushion, back foot,
  half-turn).
